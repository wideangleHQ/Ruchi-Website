## Article body

Open a pan of hot oil in many Odia kitchens and a handful of tiny seeds goes in first: they crackle, perfume the air within seconds, and become the base note of the dish to come. That small handful is **Panch Phutan** — literally "five tempering seeds" — one of the simplest and most recognisable flavour-makers in eastern Indian cooking.

### What is Panch Phutan?

Panch Phutan (also written *panch phutana* or *pancha phutana*) is a **blend of five whole spices** used for *tempering* — frying whole seeds briefly in hot oil or ghee to release their aroma, then pouring the infused oil (or adding vegetables or dal) straight on top.

It is a **whole-seed blend, not a powder**, and it is used in small quantities.

### What goes into it?

Households and brands differ slightly, but the five seeds most commonly named are:

| Seed | Common name(s) | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|
| Cumin | jeera | Warm, earthy aroma |
| Black cumin / nigella | kalajeera / kalonji | Sharp, slightly smoky, onion-like note |
| Fenugreek | methi | Pleasant bitterness; use lightly |
| Mustard | sarson / rai / sorisha | Pungent, nutty when popped |
| Fennel | saunf / madhuri | Sweet, anise-like freshness |

### Panch Phutan and panch phoron — same thing?

They are close relatives. "Panch phoron" is the name more widely used for a five-seed tempering blend in Bengali and wider eastern Indian cooking, and the Odia name Panch Phutan describes the same idea of "five tempering seeds". Exact ingredients and proportions vary by household and region, so avoid assuming that one cook's blend is identical to another's. *(Reviewer: confirm wording; avoid implying an origin claim.)*

### How to use Panch Phutan

**The basic method (tempering / "chhunk")**

1. Heat 1–2 tablespoons of oil or ghee in a pan over medium heat.
2. Add about ½ to 1 teaspoon of Panch Phutan *(adjust to the recipe and quantity of dish)*.
3. Wait for the seeds to crackle and release aroma — usually **well under a minute**.
4. Immediately add your vegetables, dal or other ingredients, or pour the tempering over a cooked dish.

**Tips**

- **Don't let it burn.** Fenugreek turns bitter if it darkens; lower the heat and move quickly.
- **Add dry red chilli or ginger to the oil with the seeds** if the recipe calls for it.
- **Keep the heat steady:** too cold and the seeds won't crackle; too hot and they scorch.
- **Salt and turmeric go in after** the seeds have bloomed.

### Where it is used

Panch Phutan is a tempering base, so it works anywhere a tempering is welcome — commonly **dals, vegetable stir-fries and mixed-vegetable curries**. It is a natural fit for Odia home-style dishes such as [Dalma](/blog/odisha-food/odia-dalma-recipe), where a final tempering lifts the whole pot. *(Reviewer: confirm the typical dishes you want to name; add 2–3 more with authentic spellings.)*

### Storing Panch Phutan

Keep it in a clean, dry, airtight container away from sunlight and heat. The RUCHI pack states **"Store in a clean & dry place away from sunlight"** and **"Best before 12 months from packaging"** — follow the date on your pack. For more detail see [how to store spices](/blog/spice-guide/how-to-store-spices).

### Make your own at home (optional)

Mix equal parts of the five seeds in a dry jar. Equal parts is a common starting point, not a rule; some cooks lighten the fenugreek because of its bitterness. *(Reviewer: confirm ratio guidance before publishing.)*

### Frequently asked questions

**Is Panch Phutan spicy?**
No, the blend is aromatic rather than hot. Heat in a dish comes from added chilli.

**Can I use it ground?**
It is designed to be used whole in hot oil; powdering it changes how it releases flavour.

**What if I don't have fenugreek or black cumin?**
You can still temper with the others, but the flavour will differ from the traditional blend.

**Does it contain any added salt or colour?**
The RUCHI pack lists only the five seeds in its ingredients — always check the pack you buy.
