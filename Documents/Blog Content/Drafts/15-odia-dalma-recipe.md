## Article body

Dalma is one of the best-loved everyday dishes of Odisha: **toor (arhar) dal cooked together with vegetables**, finished with a fragrant tempering and a sprinkle of roasted spices. It is gentle rather than fiery — comforting enough for a weeknight and a regular companion to rice.

This guide explains what Dalma is, what goes into it, and how to make a simple version at home.

### What is Dalma?

Dalma is a **lentil-and-vegetable preparation** from Odia cuisine. Unlike a plain dal, the vegetables are cooked *with* the lentils, so the dish arrives at the table thick, mildly sweet from the vegetables and lightly spiced. Recipes differ by family and region — choice of vegetables, the oil or ghee used, and the finishing spices all vary — so treat any single recipe as *one* way to make it, not the only one. *(Reviewer: confirm and enrich the cultural context.)*

### Ingredients (serves 4 — estimate)

**For the dal and vegetables**
- ¾ cup (about 150 g) toor/arhar dal, washed
- 400–500 g mixed vegetables, in large chunks — a typical mix is raw banana, pumpkin, sweet potato, brinjal and raw papaya *(use what is seasonal; reviewer to confirm a core list)*
- ½ teaspoon turmeric powder
- Salt to taste
- 3½–4 cups water, adjusted for consistency
- 2–3 tablespoons freshly grated coconut *(optional)*

**For the tempering**
- 1–2 tablespoons ghee or oil
- ½–1 teaspoon [Panch Phutan](/blog/odisha-food/panch-phutan-guide) (five-seed blend)
- 1–2 dry red chillies
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 small piece of ginger, crushed *(optional)*

**To finish**
- A pinch of roasted cumin–chilli powder (see note below)
- A little fresh coriander (optional)

> **A note on Dalma powder:** ready-made *Dalma powder* is a roasted-spice finish. The RUCHI Dalma Powder label lists **roasted cumin, roasted chilli, hing (asafoetida), turmeric and salt** — it already contains salt, so add it at the end and taste before adding more salt.

### Method

1. **Cook the dal.** Rinse the dal until the water runs mostly clear. Add it to a pot or pressure cooker with the water, turmeric and a little salt. Cook until just soft.
   *Pressure cooker: about 3–4 whistles (estimate); pot: 30–40 minutes.*
2. **Add the vegetables.** Stir in the chopped vegetables with a little more water if needed and cook until tender but still holding shape. If using a cooker, release pressure and simmer the vegetables with the dal.
3. **Adjust consistency.** Dalma should be thick but pourable. Add hot water to loosen, or mash a few pieces against the side of the pot to thicken.
4. **Prepare the tempering.** In a small pan heat the ghee or oil on medium heat. Add the Panch Phutan, red chilli and bay leaf. When the seeds crackle (a few seconds), add the ginger if using and switch off the heat — do not let the fenugreek burn.
5. **Combine.** Pour the tempering over the dal and vegetables and stir gently. Add the grated coconut if using.
6. **Finish and taste.** Sprinkle roasted cumin–chilli powder (or Dalma powder), adjust salt, and garnish with coriander.
7. **Rest for a few minutes** so the flavours settle, then serve hot.

**Timing (estimate — to be confirmed in test kitchen):** prep 20 minutes · cook 35–45 minutes · serves 4.

### Serving ideas

Dalma is most often served **with plain steamed rice**. Many households also serve it alongside a simple fried vegetable, a dry preparation or pickle. *(Reviewer: confirm authentic accompaniments and add 1–2 if appropriate.)*

### Tips for a better Dalma

- **Cut vegetables large.** They should soften without falling apart.
- **Temper last.** The aroma is strongest when the tempering goes in just before serving.
- **Watch the salt.** If you finish with a salted spice powder, add it before the final taste.
- **Thin as you like.** Add hot (not cold) water to loosen; cold water can dull the flavour.

### Common mistakes

- Burning the tempering seeds (bitter taste).
- Overcooking vegetables into mush.
- Adding the finishing powder too early so its aroma cooks away.

### Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between Dalma and regular dal?**
Dalma has vegetables cooked with the lentils, so it is a one-pot dal and vegetable dish, while a plain dal is usually lentils alone.

**Which dal is used?**
Toor (arhar) dal is the commonly used lentil. *(Reviewer: confirm; note acceptable variations.)*

**Can I make it without coconut?**
Yes — coconut is an optional addition.

**Is Dalma vegan?**
It depends on the fat used for tempering — use oil instead of ghee for a vegan version.

**Can I use Panch Phutan if I don't have it?**
You can temper with cumin and mustard, but the flavour will differ from the traditional blend.
